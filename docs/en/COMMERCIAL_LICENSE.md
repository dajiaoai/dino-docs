# Commercial License

This document explains the usage boundaries of Dino-GSP in public distribution, product embedding, and commercial cooperation scenarios.

To encourage distribution and ecosystem growth, we keep direct iframe embedding of public shared content open for use. For deeper integration scenarios such as programmatic control, private access, and branding customization, we use commercial licensing.

This document describes the current open policy and does not constitute a formal legal agreement. The final scope of authorization, pricing, term, traffic quota, and service commitments are subject to the commercial contract or written authorization signed by both parties.

## 1. Free Use Scope

The following scenarios are generally available for free use:

- Direct iframe embedding of public shared content
- Distribution-oriented scenarios such as web content display, teaching pages, blog posts, community pages, and public resource pages
- Use of the default Dino-GSP page, default branding, and default UI

Free use is subject to all of the following conditions:

- The embedded content is public shared content
- The integration uses direct iframe embedding and does not programmatically control the embedded page
- Dino-GSP domain, default branding, and default UI are retained
- No white-labeling, de-branding, private content access, batch invocation, or enterprise-grade service requirements are involved

## 2. What Free Use Does Not Include

Free use does not include the following capabilities or commercial rights:

- Programmatic control of the embedded page
- Dynamic loading or switching of shared content
- Batch generation, batch invocation, or automated processing
- Automatic canvas operations performed by AI agents, scripts, or server-side tasks
- Private content loading or restricted content access
- De-branding, white-labeling, or custom branding presentation
- Dedicated SLA, enterprise support, service commitments, or reserved resource guarantees

## 3. Scenarios That Require a Commercial License

Any of the following scenarios will generally require a commercial license:

- Calling embedded capabilities through the SDK
- Programmatically controlling the embedded page through protocol calls, scripts, message passing, or any similar mechanism
- Dynamically loading shared content, switching slides, or remotely driving canvas behavior
- Batch generation of geometry content, batch rendering, or batch task processing
- Calling geometry capabilities in AI products, agent workflows, automated problem solving, or automated tutoring scenarios
- Loading private or restricted content, or content intended for specific users
- Requiring white-labeling, de-branding, custom branding, or UI customization
- Requiring enterprise support, service guarantees, traffic quota, or other commercial rights

## 4. A Simple Rule of Thumb

If you only display a public shared page via iframe on a web page and retain Dino-GSP's default page and branding, the usage is generally free.

If your system needs to control the canvas through code, access deeper integration capabilities, or obtain private access, branding customization, automation, or enterprise guarantees, the usage will generally require a commercial license.

## 5. Typical Examples

### Examples Usually Allowed for Free Use

- A teacher embeds a publicly shared geometry canvas in a course page
- An education content platform displays publicly shared geometry content in article pages
- A community site, blog, or knowledge base embeds a public shared link through iframe

### Examples That Usually Require a Commercial License

- A teaching system dynamically loads geometry canvases for different questions through the SDK
- A question bank platform controls slide switching, file import, or REPL commands through code
- An AI product automatically calls geometry capabilities to generate figures, verify steps, or produce explanations
- A commercial product wants to hide Dino-GSP branding or present the canvas under its own brand
- An enterprise customer needs private content access control, quota guarantees, and service support

## 6. Rights Typically Covered by a Commercial License

Depending on the cooperation plan, a commercial license may cover one or more of the following rights:

- SDK and programmatic control capabilities
- Protocol-level integration capabilities
- Private content access
- White-labeling or de-branding
- Brand and UI customization
- Traffic quota and invocation limits
- Enterprise support and commercial services
